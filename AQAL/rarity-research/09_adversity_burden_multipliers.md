# Adversity/Burden Multipliers: Research Evidence

## The SAT "Adversity Score" (College Board, 2019)

The College Board created an "Environmental Context Dashboard" (later renamed "Landscape"):
- Score range: 0-100 (50 = average, higher = more disadvantage)
- Used 15 factors across neighborhood and school environments
- Factors included: crime rate, poverty rate, housing values, vacancy rate, median income, education levels, AP course availability, free lunch rates, etc.

### Key Principle: Same score + more adversity = higher TRUE ability
- A student scoring 1200 SAT from a high-adversity background (score 75) was considered equivalent to a student scoring 1350+ from a low-adversity background (score 25)
- Implicit multiplier: approximately 1.1-1.25x for high adversity

## ACE Score Impact on Cognitive Performance

Research findings on Adverse Childhood Experiences:
- Children with 4+ ACEs score approximately 0.3-0.4 SD LOWER on IQ tests (JAMA Pediatrics, 2024)
- This means: a person with IQ 115 and 4+ ACEs likely has TRUE cognitive potential of IQ 120-125
- ACE types most impactful on cognition:
  - Deprivation-type (neglect, poverty): strongest effect
  - Threat-type (abuse, violence): moderate effect
  - Household dysfunction: moderate effect

### Quantified Impact:
- 0 ACEs: baseline (no suppression)
- 1-3 ACEs: ~0.1-0.2 SD suppression on measured cognitive performance
- 4-6 ACEs: ~0.3-0.4 SD suppression
- 7+ ACEs: ~0.5+ SD suppression

## Socioeconomic Status Impact

From multiple studies:
- Low SES children score 10-15 IQ points lower than high SES children ON AVERAGE
- This gap is partially genetic but LARGELY environmental
- The "Scarr-Rowe interaction": genetic potential is MORE expressed in high-SES environments
- In low-SES environments, environmental constraints SUPPRESS genetic potential

### The Multiplier Logic:
If SES suppresses measured IQ by ~10-15 points:
- A person measuring IQ 120 from low SES likely has potential of 130-135
- This is a multiplier of approximately 1.08-1.12 on the raw score

## Hidden Talents Framework (Developmental Science)

The "Hidden Talents" framework (Ellis et al., 2022) proposes:
- People from adverse environments develop DIFFERENT cognitive strengths
- Enhanced: threat detection, attention shifting, pattern recognition in chaos
- Suppressed: sustained attention, working memory in controlled settings
- Standard tests only measure the SUPPRESSED abilities, missing the ENHANCED ones

## AQAL Burden Multiplier Framework (Proposed)

### Tier 1: Mild Burden (1.1-1.2x multiplier)
- 1-2 ACEs
- Moderate financial stress
- Single parent household (stable)
- Mild chronic health condition
- First-generation college student

### Tier 2: Moderate Burden (1.2-1.5x multiplier)
- 3-5 ACEs
- Poverty (below poverty line)
- Significant chronic illness
- Untreated ADHD/learning disability
- Immigration/language barrier
- Loss of parent before age 18
- Active caregiving responsibilities

### Tier 3: Severe Burden (1.5-2.0x multiplier)
- 6+ ACEs
- Extreme poverty + violence exposure
- Severe mental illness (managed)
- Physical disability
- Homelessness history
- Incarceration history
- Multiple simultaneous Tier 2 burdens

### Tier 4: Extraordinary Burden (2.0-3.0x multiplier)
- Combination of severe mental illness + poverty + trauma
- Active war zone/refugee status
- Severe disability + no support system
- Multiple Tier 3 burdens simultaneously

## Cross-Quadrant Burden Assessment

### Upper Left (Psychological/Neurological):
- Depression, anxiety, PTSD, bipolar, ADHD, autism (masking cost)
- Measured by: clinical diagnosis, medication history, therapy duration

### Upper Right (Biological/Physical):
- Chronic pain, disability, chronic fatigue, autoimmune conditions
- Sleep disorders, TBI history, substance recovery
- Measured by: medical history, functional limitations

### Lower Left (Cultural/Relational):
- Cultural displacement, language barriers, discrimination
- Loss of family support, abusive relationships
- Measured by: immigration status, family structure, cultural context

### Lower Right (Systemic/Societal):
- Poverty, lack of education access, geographic isolation
- Legal system involvement, lack of healthcare
- Measured by: income, education level, zip code, criminal record

## The Mathematical Application

For a person scoring at the 84th percentile (1 SD above mean) on a given dimension:
- With Tier 1 burden: adjusted to ~88th percentile (1.1x on z-score)
- With Tier 2 burden: adjusted to ~92nd percentile (1.3x on z-score)
- With Tier 3 burden: adjusted to ~95th percentile (1.7x on z-score)
- With Tier 4 burden: adjusted to ~97th percentile (2.0x on z-score)

This means: someone performing at "above average" DESPITE severe adversity is actually performing at "exceptional" relative to their true potential group.
