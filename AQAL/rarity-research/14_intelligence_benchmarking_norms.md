# Population Benchmarking Norms for 22 Intelligence Lines

## Research-Based Mean Scores and Standard Deviations

This document compiles documented population means and standard deviations from peer-reviewed research for each of the 22 intelligence lines in the AQAL framework. Where standardized instruments exist, the primary measurement tool is identified along with its scoring system.

---

## 1. FLUID REASONING (Gf)
**Instrument:** Raven's Advanced Progressive Matrices (APM) / WAIS-V Fluid Reasoning Index
**Scoring:** IQ-scaled (Mean = 100, SD = 15)
**Population Mean:** 100
**Population SD:** 15
**Source:** Raven et al. (1998); Wechsler (2024) WAIS-V Technical Manual
**Notes:** The gold standard for fluid intelligence. Scores decline ~0.3 SD per decade after age 30. College-educated adults average ~110-115 on Raven's APM.

---

## 2. CRYSTALLIZED INTELLIGENCE (Gc)
**Instrument:** WAIS-V Verbal Comprehension Index / Woodcock-Johnson IV Comprehension-Knowledge
**Scoring:** IQ-scaled (Mean = 100, SD = 15)
**Population Mean:** 100
**Population SD:** 15
**Source:** Wechsler (2024); Schrank et al. (2014) WJ-IV Technical Manual
**Notes:** Increases with age through ~65, then plateaus. Highly correlated with education level. College graduates average ~112-118.

---

## 3. MATHEMATICAL-LOGICAL INTELLIGENCE
**Instrument:** WAIS-V Quantitative Reasoning / WJ-IV Quantitative Reasoning cluster
**Scoring:** IQ-scaled (Mean = 100, SD = 15)
**Population Mean:** 100
**Population SD:** 15
**Source:** Schrank et al. (2014); McGrew et al. (2014)
**Notes:** Overlaps heavily with Gf but includes acquired mathematical knowledge. Gender differences are small (d ≈ 0.1-0.2 favoring males at tails).

---

## 4. WORKING MEMORY CAPACITY
**Instrument:** WAIS-V Working Memory Index / Operation Span (OSPAN)
**Scoring:** IQ-scaled (Mean = 100, SD = 15) for WAIS; Partial-credit unit scoring for OSPAN
**Population Mean (WAIS-V WMI):** 100
**Population SD:** 15
**OSPAN Mean (college students):** ~55-60 out of 75 (partial credit scoring)
**OSPAN SD:** ~12-15
**Source:** Redick et al. (2012); Wechsler (2024); Richmond et al. (2021)
**Notes:** OSPAN norms from 4 large state universities (2011-2019): M ≈ 57.6, SD ≈ 14.2 (N > 6,000). Declines significantly with age (~0.5 SD per decade after 50).

---

## 5. PROCESSING SPEED
**Instrument:** WAIS-V Processing Speed Index (PSI) — Digit Symbol + Symbol Search
**Scoring:** IQ-scaled (Mean = 100, SD = 15); T-scores (Mean = 50, SD = 10) for subtests
**Population Mean:** 100
**Population SD:** 15
**Source:** Wechsler (2024); Donders & Strong (2021) PMC8186464
**Notes:** Most age-sensitive cognitive ability. Peaks at ~18-25, declines ~0.4-0.5 SD per decade thereafter. T-scores range 26-79 in demographically-adjusted norms.

---

## 6. EMOTIONAL INTELLIGENCE
**Instrument:** MSCEIT (Mayer-Salovey-Caruso Emotional Intelligence Test) — ability-based; EQ-i 2.0 — self-report
**Scoring:** MSCEIT: Standard score (Mean = 100, SD = 15); EQ-i 2.0: Standard score (Mean = 100, SD = 15)
**Population Mean (MSCEIT):** 100
**Population SD:** 15
**Population Mean (EQ-i 2.0 Total):** 100
**Population SD:** 15
**Source:** Mayer et al. (2002) MSCEIT Manual; Multi-Health Systems (2011) EQ-i 2.0 Technical Manual
**Notes:** MSCEIT normed on N = 5,000+ diverse adults. Females score ~0.5 SD higher on MSCEIT. EQ-i 2.0 normed on N = 4,000+. Subscales: Self-Perception, Self-Expression, Interpersonal, Decision Making, Stress Management all M=100, SD=15.

---

## 7. INTRAPERSONAL INTELLIGENCE (Self-Awareness)
**Instrument:** EQ-i 2.0 Self-Perception Composite / NEO-PI-R Openness to Feelings facet
**Scoring:** EQ-i: Standard score (Mean = 100, SD = 15); NEO-PI-R: T-score (Mean = 50, SD = 10)
**Population Mean (EQ-i Self-Perception):** 100
**Population SD:** 15
**NEO-PI-R Openness to Feelings Mean:** T = 50
**NEO-PI-R SD:** 10
**Source:** Multi-Health Systems (2011); Costa & McCrae (1992) NEO-PI-R Professional Manual
**Notes:** Self-Perception composite includes Self-Regard, Self-Actualization, Emotional Self-Awareness. NEO Openness to Feelings: raw score M ≈ 23.5, SD ≈ 4.8 (combined gender norms, N = 1,000).

---

## 8. SELF-REGULATION / SELF-CONTROL
**Instrument:** Brief Self-Control Scale (BSCS; Tangney et al., 2004)
**Scoring:** 13 items, 5-point Likert (1-5). Total range: 13-65.
**Population Mean:** ~39.0 (3.0 per item average)
**Population SD:** ~8.5
**Source:** Tangney et al. (2004) original validation (N = 351 undergraduates, M = 3.15/item); Fung et al. (2020) Chinese sample (N = 903, Cronbach's α = 0.81); de Ridder et al. (2012) meta-analysis
**Notes:** Higher scores = better self-control. Mean per item typically 2.9-3.2 across cultures. Gender differences minimal. Correlates r = 0.34 with GPA, r = -0.42 with psychopathology symptoms.

---

## 9. MORAL/ETHICAL REASONING
**Instrument:** Defining Issues Test-2 (DIT-2; Rest et al., 1999)
**Scoring:** N2 score (continuous, typically 0-95 range)
**Population Mean (general adults):** ~30-35
**Population SD:** ~15
**Population Mean (college students):** ~35-40
**Population SD:** ~14-16
**Source:** Rest et al. (1999); Gungordu et al. (2024) "Moral reasoning development: norms for DIT-2"; Salisbury University (2019) N=1,769 students: M=30.0
**Notes:** N2 score increases with education: High school ~25, College ~35, Graduate/Professional ~45, Moral philosophers ~65. Postconventional (P) score: general adults M ≈ 30-40%, SD ≈ 15%.

---

## 10. INTERPERSONAL INTELLIGENCE (Empathy/Social Cognition)
**Instrument:** Interpersonal Reactivity Index (IRI; Davis, 1980, 1983)
**Scoring:** 4 subscales, 7 items each, 5-point Likert (0-4). Subscale range: 0-28.
**Population Means (combined gender):**
- Perspective Taking (PT): M ≈ 17.5, SD ≈ 4.5
- Empathic Concern (EC): M ≈ 19.5, SD ≈ 4.3
- Fantasy (FS): M ≈ 16.0, SD ≈ 5.5
- Personal Distress (PD): M ≈ 11.5, SD ≈ 5.0
**Source:** Davis (1980, 1983) original norms; Fetzer Institute compilation; Italian norms (Santangelo et al., 2022, N=1,049)
**Notes:** Females score higher on EC (~21 vs ~18) and PD (~13 vs ~10). PT and EC are the "healthy empathy" subscales. Total empathy (PT+EC) M ≈ 37, SD ≈ 7.

---

## 11. SOCIAL INTELLIGENCE (Interpersonal Effectiveness)
**Instrument:** Tromsø Social Intelligence Scale (TSIS; Silvera et al., 2001)
**Scoring:** 21 items, 7-point Likert (1-7). Three subscales: Social Information Processing, Social Skills, Social Awareness.
**Population Mean (Total):** ~95-100 (out of 147)
**Population SD:** ~15-18
**Subscale Means:** Social Information Processing M ≈ 35, Social Skills M ≈ 32, Social Awareness M ≈ 30
**Source:** Silvera et al. (2001) original validation; Grieve & Mahar (2013)
**Notes:** Limited large-scale norming. College student samples show M ≈ 4.5-4.8 per item. Correlates r ≈ 0.35 with EQ-i Interpersonal composite.

---

## 12. LEADERSHIP/ORGANIZATIONAL INTELLIGENCE
**Instrument:** Multifactor Leadership Questionnaire (MLQ 5X; Bass & Avolio, 2004)
**Scoring:** 45 items, 5-point Likert (0-4). Key subscale: Transformational Leadership (20 items, range 0-80).
**Population Mean (Transformational Leadership):** ~2.7-3.0 per item (self-rating)
**Population SD:** ~0.6-0.8 per item
**Source:** Bass & Avolio (2004) MLQ Manual; Antonakis et al. (2003); Polish validation (Harasymczuk et al., 2022, Frontiers)
**Notes:** Self-ratings typically 0.5 higher than subordinate ratings. Transformational Leadership mean: leaders M ≈ 3.0, non-leaders M ≈ 2.4. Normed on N > 27,000 across industries.

---

## 13. COMMUNICATION/LINGUISTIC INTELLIGENCE
**Instrument:** Self-Perceived Communication Competence Scale (SPCC; McCroskey & McCroskey, 1988)
**Scoring:** 12 items, 0-100 scale each. Total: average of all items (0-100).
**Population Mean:** ~67.5
**Population SD:** ~13.0
**Source:** McCroskey & McCroskey (1988) original norms; longitudinal analysis (Wrench et al., 2024)
**Notes:** Norms: Mean = 67.5, SD = 13.0 (combined college + adult non-student samples). Scores >80 = high SPCC. Scores <55 = low SPCC. Stutterers score significantly lower (M ≈ 55). Subscales: Public (M ≈ 60), Meeting (M ≈ 68), Group (M ≈ 70), Dyad (M ≈ 78).

---

## 14. KINESTHETIC/BODILY INTELLIGENCE
**Instrument:** Body Intelligence Scale (BIS; Anderson, 2006) / No universally standardized instrument
**Scoring:** BIS: 25 items, 5-point Likert. Range: 25-125.
**Population Mean (estimated):** ~75-80 (3.0-3.2 per item)
**Population SD (estimated):** ~12-15
**Source:** Anderson (2006) "Body Intelligence Scale: Defining and measuring the intelligence of the body"; Chenini & Merzougui (2026)
**Notes:** LEAST standardized of all 22 lines. No large-scale population norming exists. Proxy measures: Physical Activity Readiness, motor coordination tests, proprioception accuracy. Athletes score ~1-1.5 SD above general population on motor tasks. Gardner's framework identifies this but no IQ-equivalent scale exists.

---

## 15. SPATIAL/VISUAL INTELLIGENCE
**Instrument:** WAIS-V Visual Spatial Index / Mental Rotation Test (MRT; Vandenberg & Kuse, 1978)
**Scoring:** WAIS-V VSI: IQ-scaled (Mean = 100, SD = 15); MRT: 0-24 correct (redrawn version)
**Population Mean (WAIS-V VSI):** 100
**Population SD:** 15
**MRT Mean (college students):** Males M ≈ 15.0, Females M ≈ 10.5 (out of 24)
**MRT SD:** Males SD ≈ 5.0, Females SD ≈ 4.5
**Source:** Wechsler (2024); Peters et al. (1995) "A redrawn Vandenberg and Kuse MRT" (N = 3,367)
**Notes:** Largest gender difference of any cognitive ability (d ≈ 0.9 on MRT). WAIS-V VSI gender difference smaller (d ≈ 0.2). Highly trainable — practice effects of 0.5-1.0 SD documented.

---

## 16. MUSICAL/RHYTHMIC INTELLIGENCE
**Instrument:** Advanced Measures of Music Audiation (AMMA; Gordon, 1989)
**Scoring:** Tonal (1-40), Rhythm (1-40), Composite (2-80). Raw scores converted to percentile ranks.
**Population Mean (college non-music majors):** Tonal M ≈ 26, Rhythm M ≈ 27, Composite M ≈ 53
**Population SD:** Tonal SD ≈ 5.5, Rhythm SD ≈ 5.0, Composite SD ≈ 9.5
**Music majors:** Tonal M ≈ 32, Rhythm M ≈ 32, Composite M ≈ 64
**Source:** Gordon (1989) AMMA Manual; Demorest et al. (2022); GIA Publications norms
**Notes:** Normed on N > 5,000 college students. Music majors score ~1 SD above non-majors. Test-retest reliability: r = 0.82-0.85. Measures "audiation" (inner hearing) not performance skill.

---

## 17. NATURALISTIC INTELLIGENCE
**Instrument:** Nature Relatedness Scale-6 (NR-6; Nisbet & Zelenski, 2013)
**Scoring:** 6 items, 5-point Likert (1-5). Mean score range: 1-5.
**Population Mean:** 3.31
**Population SD:** 0.86
**Source:** Nisbet & Zelenski (2013) original (N=184, M=3.00, SD=0.86); Richardson et al. (2018) UK (N=310, M=3.57, SD=0.92); OpenPsychometrics weighted average across 7 studies
**Notes:** Online samples score higher (M ≈ 4.08) due to self-selection bias. Indigenous populations score highest (M ≈ 3.68). Positively correlated with well-being (r ≈ 0.20) and negatively with anxiety.

---

## 18. EXISTENTIAL/SPIRITUAL INTELLIGENCE
**Instrument:** Spiritual Intelligence Self-Report Inventory (SISRI-24; King, 2008)
**Scoring:** 24 items, 5-point Likert (0-4). Total range: 0-96.
**Population Mean:** ~55-58
**Population SD:** ~16-18
**Source:** King (2008) original validation; Greek adaptation (Zartaloudi et al.): M=55.7, SD=17.4; Polish adaptation (Atroszko et al., 2021, N=833)
**Notes:** Four subscales: Critical Existential Thinking, Personal Meaning Production, Transcendental Awareness, Conscious State Expansion. Factor structure is debated — Polish study found single general factor inadequate. Religious populations score ~0.5 SD higher.

---

## 19. CREATIVE/GENERATIVE INTELLIGENCE
**Instrument:** Torrance Tests of Creative Thinking — Figural (TTCT-F; Torrance, 1966/2008)
**Scoring:** Standard scores (Mean = 100, SD = 20) for each dimension; Creativity Index composite
**Population Mean:** 100
**Population SD:** 20
**Source:** Torrance (2008) TTCT Norms-Technical Manual; Kim (2011) "The creativity crisis"; Washburn University (2018) Grade 13: M=93.6
**Notes:** Five norm-referenced measures: Fluency, Originality, Abstractness of Titles, Elaboration, Resistance to Premature Closure. Plus 13 criterion-referenced "creative strengths." Creativity Index is the composite. Scores have DECLINED since 1990 (the "creativity crisis"). SD=20 (wider than IQ's SD=15) reflects greater variability.

---

## 20. SYSTEMS THINKING
**Instrument:** Systems Thinking Scale (STS; Moore et al., 2010/2020)
**Scoring:** 20 items, 5-point Likert (0-4). Total range: 0-80.
**Population Mean (healthcare professionals):** 54.5
**Population SD:** 7.3
**Source:** Moore et al. (2020) "Development and Validation of the Systems Thinking Scale" (N=741 nurses); Dolansky et al. (2017) multisite study
**Notes:** Normed primarily on healthcare professionals. Higher STS scores predict better error reporting (OR=1.05). No general population norms exist — healthcare workers likely score higher than general public. Estimated general population: M ≈ 48-52, SD ≈ 8-10.

---

## 21. AESTHETIC INTELLIGENCE
**Instrument:** NEO-PI-R Aesthetics facet (O2) / AESTHEMOS (Aesthetic Emotions Scale)
**Scoring:** NEO-PI-R O2: T-score (Mean = 50, SD = 10); raw score 8 items, range 0-32
**Population Mean (NEO-PI-R Aesthetics T-score):** 50
**Population SD:** 10
**Raw score Mean:** ~20.5
**Raw SD:** ~5.5
**Source:** Costa & McCrae (1992) NEO-PI-R Manual (N=1,000); Schindler et al. (2017) AESTHEMOS
**Notes:** Aesthetics facet of Openness to Experience. Higher in artists, musicians, writers (~1 SD above mean). Gender difference: females slightly higher (d ≈ 0.15). Correlates r ≈ 0.45 with creative achievement.

---

## 22. ADAPTIVE/PRACTICAL INTELLIGENCE
**Instrument:** Tacit Knowledge Inventories (Sternberg et al., 2000) / No single standardized instrument
**Scoring:** Varies by inventory. Typically scenario-based, rated on quality of response.
**Population Mean:** Not standardized — no IQ-equivalent scale exists
**Population SD:** Not established
**Source:** Sternberg et al. (2000) "Practical Intelligence in Everyday Life"; Wagner & Sternberg (1985); Hedlund et al. (2006)
**Notes:** MOST CONTROVERSIAL of all intelligence lines. Sternberg's STAT (Sternberg Triarchic Abilities Test) practical subscale exists but lacks population norms. Tacit knowledge inventories are domain-specific (military, business, academic). Correlates only r ≈ 0.1-0.2 with IQ, supporting discriminant validity. Proxy: ABAS-3 General Adaptive Composite (Mean = 100, SD = 15) measures adaptive behavior but is designed for disability assessment.

## KEY FINDINGS FOR AQAL RARITY CALCULATION

### 1. Standardization Advantage
- 10 of 22 lines have IQ-equivalent scaling (Mean=100, SD=15), making rarity calculation straightforward
- 5 lines use T-scores or standard scores with known distributions
- 7 lines use Likert scales that must be converted to z-scores before rarity calculation

### 2. Conversion Formula
For any raw score on any instrument:
```
z = (individual_score - population_mean) / population_SD
```
Then convert z to percentile/rarity:
- z = 1.0 → top 16% (1 in 6)
- z = 1.5 → top 7% (1 in 15)
- z = 2.0 → top 2.3% (1 in 44)
- z = 2.5 → top 0.6% (1 in 160)
- z = 3.0 → top 0.13% (1 in 741)

### 3. Inter-Correlation Matrix (Estimated from Literature)
The effective dimensionality depends on correlations between lines:
- Within-cognitive cluster (lines 1-5): r ≈ 0.5-0.7 (HIGH correlation)
- Within-emotional cluster (lines 6-10): r ≈ 0.3-0.5 (MODERATE)
- Within-social cluster (lines 10-13): r ≈ 0.3-0.4 (MODERATE)
- Cross-domain (cognitive ↔ emotional): r ≈ 0.1-0.3 (LOW)
- Cross-domain (cognitive ↔ kinesthetic): r ≈ 0.0-0.2 (VERY LOW)
- Cross-domain (emotional ↔ spiritual): r ≈ 0.2-0.4 (LOW-MODERATE)

### 4. Effective Independent Dimensions
Given the correlation structure, 22 measured lines collapse to approximately **5-7 effective independent dimensions** for rarity calculation. This is our multiplier base.

### 5. Gaps and Limitations
- **Kinesthetic (14):** No standardized population norms. Must use proxy measures or self-report.
- **Adaptive/Practical (22):** No standardized instrument with population norms. Most controversial construct.
- **Systems Thinking (20):** Only normed on healthcare professionals, not general population.
- **Social Intelligence (11):** Limited norming samples (<500 in most studies).
- **Spiritual (18):** Factor structure debated; measurement validity questioned.

### 6. Recommendation for AQAL
For lines WITHOUT IQ-equivalent norms, use the following approach:
1. Collect self-report data from first 1,000 users
2. Fit distribution to collected data
3. Use percentile rank within OUR sample as interim norm
4. Update continuously as sample grows (continuous norming)
5. Cross-validate against established instruments when possible

This gives us a defensible starting point while we build our own normative database.
