## Key Mathematical Foundation

All use the SAME formula:
- Normal distribution: Mean = 100, SD = 15
- z-score = (IQ - 100) / 15
- Percentile = CDF(z)
- Rarity = 1 / (1 - Percentile)

## Ceiling Effects in IQ Testing

Critical limitation: Most IQ tests cap at 160 (WAIS-IV) or 165 (Stanford-Binet 5)
- Above 160, you cannot reliably distinguish between individuals
- Norming samples are too small at the extremes
- This is why ratio IQ (mental age / chronological age × 100) was abandoned

## IMPLICATION FOR AQAL MODEL

Traditional IQ has a CEILING PROBLEM. It can't differentiate above ~160 (1 in 31,000).
Our model SOLVES this by measuring across 22 dimensions:
- Even if each dimension caps at "top 5%", the COMBINATION creates extreme rarity
- 22 dimensions × continuous scoring = effectively unlimited ceiling
- We can differentiate between people that IQ tests cannot
