# Global IQ Tests: Structure, Scoring, and Norming Comparison

## 1. EUROPEAN TESTS

### A. Raven's Progressive Matrices (UK, 1938)
- **Creator:** John C. Raven, University of Edinburgh
- **What it measures:** Pure fluid intelligence (Gf) — nonverbal abstract reasoning
- **Structure:** 
  - SPM (Standard): 60 items, 5 sets of 12 (Sets A-E), increasing difficulty
  - APM (Advanced): 36 items in 2 sets — Set I (12 practice), Set II (36 items)
  - CPM (Coloured): 36 items for children/elderly
- **Subtests/Dimensions:** ONLY ONE — pattern completion/matrix reasoning
  - No verbal, no numerical, no memory, no speed
  - Pure measure of "eductive ability" (making meaning from confusion)
- **Scoring:** Raw score → percentile rank using age-stratified norms
  - Uses percentile bands: 95th+, 90th, 75th, 50th, 25th, 10th, 5th-
  - IQ equivalent derived via normal distribution assumption (mean 100, SD 15)
- **Norming:** Standardized across 20+ countries with local norms
  - UK norms: 3,500+ participants stratified by age
  - US norms: Separate standardization samples
- **Intelligence lines measured:** 1 (fluid reasoning only)
- **Rarity determination:** Percentile rank from normative tables; assumes normal distribution for IQ conversion

### B. Cattell Culture Fair Intelligence Test (CFIT/CFT) (UK, 1949)
- **Creator:** Raymond B. Cattell
- **What it measures:** Fluid intelligence (Gf) exclusively — culture-reduced
- **Structure:** 3 Scales with 4 subtests each
  - Scale 1: Ages 4-8 (basic)
  - Scale 2: Ages 8-13, average adults (moderate)
  - Scale 3: Above-average adults (advanced)
- **4 Subtests per scale:**
  1. Series Completion (inductive reasoning, pattern detection)
  2. Classification (categorical reasoning)
  3. Matrices (spatial reasoning, pattern completion)
  4. Conditions/Topology (spatial intelligence, abstract spatial concepts)
- **Scoring:** Mean 100, SD **16** (NOT 15 — unique to CFIT)
  - Raw → standardized using age-appropriate norms
  - Parallel forms A and B for retesting
- **Norming:** International samples; accepted by Mensa worldwide
- **Intelligence lines measured:** 1 (fluid reasoning via 4 different task formats)
- **Key difference:** SD of 16 means scores spread wider than WAIS/SB5
- **Reliability:** Internal consistency .70-.85; test-retest .70-.82

### C. IST-2000R (Intelligenz-Struktur-Test) (Germany, 2007)
- **Creator:** Liepmann et al., based on Thurstone + Cattell-Horn theory
- **What it measures:** Both fluid AND crystallized intelligence
- **Structure:** 9 subtests measuring reasoning + knowledge
  - **Reasoning Module (Fluid):**
    - 3 Verbal reasoning subtests
    - 3 Numerical reasoning subtests
    - 3 Figural reasoning subtests
  - **Knowledge Module (Crystallized):**
    - 84 tasks equally divided: verbal, numerical, figural knowledge
- **Scoring:** Composite scores for:
  - General Reasoning Ability (fluid)
  - General Knowledge (crystallized)
  - Domain-specific scores (verbal, numerical, figural)
- **Norming:** German-speaking populations; adapted to Latvia, other European countries
  - Latvian adaptation: 1,017 participants aged 15-65
- **Intelligence lines measured:** 6 (3 reasoning × content + 3 knowledge × content)
- **Unique feature:** Separates reasoning ability from acquired knowledge

### D. BIS-4 (Berliner Intelligenzstrukturmodell) (Germany)
- **Creator:** Adolf Otto Jäger (1982)
- **What it measures:** Hierarchical + faceted intelligence model
- **Structure:** Cross-classification of:
  - **4 Operations:** Reasoning, Memory, Speed, Creativity
  - **3 Content facets:** Verbal, Figural, Numerical
  - = 12 cells, each with multiple tasks
- **Scoring:** 
  - 12 cell scores (operation × content)
  - 4 operation composite scores
  - 3 content composite scores
  - 1 general intelligence (g) score
- **Intelligence lines measured:** 12 (4 operations × 3 content areas)
- **Unique feature:** Most dimensionally rich European IQ test
  - Includes CREATIVITY as a measured operation (unique among IQ tests)
  - BIS-HB version specifically for gifted assessment

## 2. AMERICAN TESTS

### A. WAIS-V (Wechsler Adult Intelligence Scale, 5th Ed.) (USA, 2024)
- **Creator:** David Wechsler (original 1939); Pearson Clinical (current)
- **What it measures:** General cognitive ability across multiple domains
- **Structure:** 7 core subtests (reduced from 10 in WAIS-IV) + supplemental
  - **4 Index Scores:**
    1. Verbal Comprehension (VCI): Similarities, Vocabulary, Information
    2. Perceptual Reasoning (PRI): Block Design, Matrix Reasoning, Visual Puzzles
    3. Working Memory (WMI): Digit Span, Arithmetic
    4. Processing Speed (PSI): Symbol Search, Coding
  - Full-Scale IQ (FSIQ) from all core subtests
- **Scoring:** 
  - Subtest raw → scaled scores (mean 10, SD 3) using age-based norms
  - Scaled scores summed → Index scores (mean 100, SD 15)
  - All indices → FSIQ (mean 100, SD 15)
- **Norming (2024):**
  - 2,200+ U.S. adults aged 16-90
  - Stratified on: age (13 bands), sex, race/ethnicity, education, geographic region
  - Matched to most current U.S. Census data (2023-2024 collection)
- **Intelligence lines measured:** 4 indices, but ~10 narrow abilities via subtests
- **Reliability:** Composite scores .90-.98; FSIQ typically .95+
- **Rarity determination:** Percentile from normal distribution; confidence intervals provided

### B. Stanford-Binet 5 (SB5) (USA, 2003)
- **Creator:** Lewis Terman (original 1916); Gale Roid (SB5)
- **What it measures:** 5 CHC cognitive factors, verbal + nonverbal
- **Structure:** 10 subtests (5 factors × 2 modalities)
  - **5 Factors:**
    1. Fluid Reasoning (FR)
    2. Knowledge (KN) — crystallized
    3. Quantitative Reasoning (QR)
    4. Visual-Spatial Processing (VS)
    5. Working Memory (WM)
  - Each factor has: 1 Verbal testlet + 1 Nonverbal testlet
- **Scoring:**
  - Full Scale IQ (FSIQ)
  - Verbal IQ composite
  - Nonverbal IQ composite
  - 5 Factor Index scores
  - All on mean 100, SD 15
- **Norming:**
  - 4,800 individuals aged 2-85+
  - Largest standardization sample of major IQ tests
  - Stratified to U.S. Census
- **Intelligence lines measured:** 5 factors × 2 modalities = 10 testlets
- **Unique feature:** Adaptive testing — adjusts difficulty to examinee level
  - Wide age range (2 to 85+)
  - Extended IQ range for gifted assessment

### C. Woodcock-Johnson IV (WJ-IV COG) (USA, 2014)
- **Creator:** Richard Woodcock; Riverside Insights
- **What it measures:** Most comprehensive CHC-based battery
- **Structure:** 18 subtests measuring:
  - **7 CHC Broad Abilities:**
    1. Comprehension-Knowledge (Gc) — crystallized
    2. Fluid Reasoning (Gf)
    3. Short-Term Working Memory (Gwm)
    4. Cognitive Processing Speed (Gs)
    5. Auditory Processing (Ga)
    6. Long-Term Retrieval (Glr)
    7. Visual Processing (Gv)
  - Plus numerous narrow abilities within each broad ability
- **Scoring:**
  - General Intellectual Ability (GIA) — weighted composite
  - Brief Intellectual Ability (BIA) — 3-test quick version
  - 7 Broad Ability cluster scores
  - Individual subtest scores
  - All on mean 100, SD 15
- **Norming:** Large nationally representative U.S. sample
- **Intelligence lines measured:** 7 broad + 18+ narrow abilities
- **Unique feature:** Most CHC-aligned; measures auditory processing and long-term retrieval (unique among major batteries)

## 3. MILITARY APTITUDE TESTS

### A. ASVAB / AFQT (USA)
- **What it measures:** Cognitive aptitude for military service
- **Structure:** 10 subtests:
  1. General Science (GS)
  2. Arithmetic Reasoning (AR)
  3. Word Knowledge (WK)
  4. Paragraph Comprehension (PC)
  5. Mathematics Knowledge (MK)
  6. Electronics Information (EI)
  7. Auto & Shop Information (AS)
  8. Mechanical Comprehension (MC)
  9. Assembling Objects (AO)
  10. Verbal Expression (VE = WK + PC)
- **AFQT Score:** Composite of AR + MK + PC + WK
  - Reported as percentiles (1-99)
  - Reference group: 18-23 year olds from 1997 national norming study
- **Scoring method:** Item Response Theory (3PL model)
  - Standard scores: mean 50, SD 10 per subtest
  - Computerized Adaptive Testing (CAT) version adjusts difficulty
- **AFQT Categories:**
  - Cat I: 93-99 percentile (top ~7%)
  - Cat II: 65-92 percentile
  - Cat IIIA: 50-64 percentile
  - Cat IIIB: 31-49 percentile
  - Cat IV: 10-30 percentile
  - Cat V: 1-9 percentile (ineligible for service)
- **Intelligence lines measured:** 4 core (verbal, math, reasoning, comprehension) + 6 technical
- **Rarity:** Percentile-based; Cat I ≈ IQ 120+

## 4. ASIAN TESTS

### A. Tanaka-Binet Intelligence Scale (Japan)
- **What it measures:** Modified Binet-style intelligence for Japanese population
- **Structure:** Age-graded tasks similar to Stanford-Binet
  - Tanaka B: Group administration version
- **Norming:** Japanese population samples
- **Note:** Japan also uses WISC/WAIS (Japanese adaptations) extensively

### B. Chinese IQ Testing
- **Primary instruments:** 
  - Chinese Wechsler Intelligence Scale (C-WAIS) — adapted WAIS
  - Raven's Progressive Matrices (widely used in China)
  - Combined Raven's Test (CRT) — Chinese adaptation
- **Norming:** Chinese population samples; separate urban/rural norms in some versions

## 6. THE NORMING/UNDERWRITING PROCESS (How They Establish Rarity)

### Step-by-Step Process Used by ALL Major Tests:

**Step 1: Construct Specification**
- Define exactly what is being measured (blueprint document)
- Specify target population and intended use

**Step 2: Item Development**
- Write 3-5x more items than final test
- Expert review for content, bias, cultural loading
- Pilot with 200-500 participants

**Step 3: Item Calibration**
- Classical Test Theory (CTT): p-values, item-total correlations
- Item Response Theory (IRT): difficulty (b), discrimination (a), guessing (c) parameters
- Differential Item Functioning (DIF) analysis for fairness
- Drop items that are too easy (>90%), too hard (<10%), or biased

**Step 4: Standardization Sample**
- WAIS-IV: 2,200 adults stratified to U.S. Census
- SB5: 4,800 individuals
- Stratification variables: age, sex, race/ethnicity, education, geography
- Need ~100 cases above 99th percentile for tail precision → requires 2,000+ total

**Step 5: Score Conversion (The Rarity Formula)**
1. Rank all participants by raw score within each age band
2. Convert rank to percentile
3. Convert percentile to z-score via inverse normal CDF
4. Linear transform: **IQ = 100 + 15 × z**
5. This FORCES a normal distribution with known rarity properties

**Step 6: Reliability & Validity**
- Internal consistency: Cronbach's α ≥ .90 for composites
- Test-retest: r ≥ .85 over weeks-months
- Construct validity: Factor analysis confirms structure
- Criterion validity: Correlates with academic/occupational outcomes

**Step 7: Re-norming**
- Every 7-15 years (Flynn effect: ~3 IQ points/decade drift)
- WAIS-IV (2008) → WAIS-V (2024) = 16 years

### The Rarity Assumption:
ALL major IQ tests assume scores follow a **normal (Gaussian) distribution**. This means:
- 68.2% score between 85-115
- 95.4% score between 70-130
- 99.7% score between 55-145
- 1 in 50 = IQ 130+
- 1 in 1,000 = IQ 145+
- 1 in 30,000 = IQ 160+
- 1 in 1,000,000 = IQ 176+

**Critical limitation:** The normal distribution assumption breaks down at extremes (>3 SD). Real distributions may have fatter or thinner tails. This is why scores above 160 are controversial — there simply aren't enough people in standardization samples to validate those claims.
