## Story #22: The Flying Squirrel & The Animal Lady

**The Story:** Sam was walking at Winterthur (one of the top gardens in the country, Wilmington, Delaware) in the summertime. 45 minutes into the walk, he found a flying squirrel on the path walking in circles — something was wrong with it. He picked it up and carried it 45 minutes back to his car. Brought it home. Fed it carrots and celery. Next day, called animal control — they said there's nothing they can do, call "the animal lady in Wilmington." Gave him a number. He drove to her house the next day. When he got out of the car with the squirrel in his hand, she was standing on her porch. She said: "Stay." He kept walking. "Sit. Stay." He didn't absorb it. She said it seven times before he realized she was talking to HIM — like a dog. She was so used to animals that she commanded humans the same way. She brought him to the backyard, into a barn with ~50 animals, including other flying squirrels. She said the squirrel wasn't looking good but she'd do her best. He left it with her. "Nobody's ever told me to sit or to stay."

**Emotions Elicited:** Warmth, Laughter, Endearment, Caretaking Signal

**Why It Works (Four Layers):**

1. **Caretaking depth:** He carried a wild animal 45 minutes back to his car. Fed it. Called around. Drove to a stranger's house. Zero obligation. Zero reward. Pure heart. This is the THIRD caretaking story (Super Cat, Silver Coins, now this) — it stops being a story and becomes a PATTERN. She thinks: "This is just who he is."

2. **Comedy gold:** The animal lady telling him to "Sit. Stay." SEVEN TIMES. The image of Sam walking toward her, focused on the squirrel, while she's barking commands at him like he's a golden retriever — and him not even registering it. This is the detail she retells to her sister word for word.

3. **Setting paints a picture:** Winterthur is gorgeous. She knows it (Delaware Catholic, probably been there). "One of the top gardens in the country" — he walks in beautiful places alone. He notices things. He's present.

4. **Vulnerability through obliviousness:** He didn't realize he was being talked to like a dog for SEVEN commands. This is endearing — he was so focused on helping the animal that he wasn't even processing his own environment. It's the opposite of self-conscious. It's pure.

**Lesson Options:** "I don't walk past things that need help. Even if it costs me 45 minutes and a trip to a stranger's barn." / "She talked to me like I was a dog for seven commands before I noticed. I was too busy holding a squirrel to care about my dignity."

**Question Options:** "Ever been so focused on helping something that you forgot to notice how ridiculous you looked?" / "What's the strangest thing you've ever done for something that couldn't thank you?"
