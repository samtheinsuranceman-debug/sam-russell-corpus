## 1.4 What the base already has that the donors do not

Worth stating plainly, because it reframes the whole exercise. The base is not the small
build; on every dimension that carries logic, it is the large one.

- **shared/ 187 modules vs 63.** The base holds `altCredit/` (a 10,000-run credit-cycle Monte
  Carlo with a term-mismatch model), `householdGenome`, `whispererEngine`, `sequencePlanner`,
  `mechanismDossiers`, `policyMechanics`, `irc7702`, `zipEngine`, `careerEngine`,
  `liquidityRoutes`, `mortgageLedger` — none of which exist in either donor.
- **server/ 147 modules vs 49**, including 29 routers the donors lack.
- **155 schema tables vs 116.**
- **A route manifest** (`shared/routeManifest.ts`) with a reconcile script and smoke tests
  that fail with the specific differing paths named in both directions. Neither donor has
  governance of this kind.
- **A page audit corpus** (`audit/full_page_audit_corpus.json`, `audit/page_inputs/*.json`) —
  per-page structured audit data.

The donors lead in exactly three places: the gamification set, 7 schema tables, and the
Postgres/Vercel deployment pattern.
