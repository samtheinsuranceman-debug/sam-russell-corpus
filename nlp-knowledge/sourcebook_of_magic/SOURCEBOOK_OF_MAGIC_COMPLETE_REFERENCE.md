## THE 77 PATTERNS

### Category 1: BASIC PATTERNS (Foundational Meta-Patterns)
*These are the patterns you run INSIDE other patterns. Master these first.*

**#1 Well-Formed Outcome Pattern**
Purpose: Establish clear, achievable goals that meet all neurological criteria for success.
Steps:
1. State what you want (positive terms, not what you don't want)
2. Specify the outcome in sensory-based terms (what will you see, hear, feel?)
3. Determine if it's self-initiated and self-maintained
4. Identify the context (where, when, with whom?)
5. Check that it's appropriately sized (not too big, not too small)
6. Identify the resources needed
7. Check ecology (does this fit with your other goals and values?)

**#2 Pacing or Matching Another's Model of the World**
Purpose: Build rapport by matching another person's representational system, physiology, and language patterns.
Steps:
1. Observe the person's breathing, posture, gestures, voice tone/tempo
2. Match their predicates (visual, auditory, kinesthetic language)
3. Mirror their physiology subtly
4. Match their energy level and speaking pace
5. Verify rapport by leading slightly — if they follow, rapport is established

**#3 Calibration to Someone's State**
Purpose: Develop sensory acuity to detect internal state changes in others.
Steps:
1. Ask the person to think of something pleasant — observe their physiology
2. Break state
3. Ask them to think of something unpleasant — observe changes
4. Note differences in: skin color, muscle tension, breathing, lip size, eye focus
5. Practice until you can detect state shifts without asking

**#4 Checking the Ecology of a Pattern**
Purpose: Ensure any change fits harmoniously with the person's whole system.
Steps:
1. Ask: "If you could have this change right now, would you take it?"
2. Check for incongruence (hesitation, voice shift, asymmetric gestures)
3. Ask: "Does any part of you object to this change?"
4. If objections arise, address them before proceeding
5. Future pace: "Imagine having this change 6 months from now — what do you notice?"

**#5 Flexibility of Responses**
Purpose: Generate multiple behavioral choices in any situation.
Steps:
1. Identify the stuck state or limited response
2. Generate at least 3 alternative responses
3. Try on each response in your imagination
4. Notice which gives you the most resourceful feelings
5. Rehearse the new responses until they become automatic

**#6 State Elicitation**
Purpose: Help someone access a specific emotional/mental state.
Steps:
1. Ask them to recall a time when they fully experienced the desired state
2. As they access it, have them step fully into the memory (associated)
3. Amplify: "Make the colors brighter, sounds louder, feelings stronger"
4. Anchor the state at its peak
5. Break state, then test the anchor

**#7 State Induction**
Purpose: Lead someone into a specific state through your own state and language.
Steps:
1. First access the state yourself (you can't lead where you haven't been)
2. Use language patterns that presuppose the state
3. Match and lead their physiology toward the state
4. Use embedded commands and tonal shifts
5. Calibrate to confirm they've entered the state

**#8 State Interrupt**
Purpose: Break an unresourceful state pattern.
Steps:
1. Identify the unresourceful state
2. Do something unexpected (change topic, ask a non-sequitur, change physiology)
3. The interrupt must be strong enough to break the pattern
4. Immediately redirect to a more resourceful state
5. Common interrupts: humor, movement, confusion, surprise

**#9 Anchoring**
Purpose: Connect a specific stimulus to a specific state for instant access.
Steps:
1. Elicit a powerful state (see #6)
2. At the peak of the state, apply a unique stimulus (touch, word, gesture)
3. Break state
4. Test: fire the anchor — does the state return?
5. If weak, stack multiple instances of the state on the same anchor
6. For lasting anchors: use unique stimuli, fire at peak intensity, repeat

**#10 Accessing Positive Intention**
Purpose: Find the positive purpose behind any behavior, even problematic ones.
Steps:
1. Identify the behavior you want to understand
2. Ask: "What does this behavior do for you?" or "What do you gain from this?"
3. Keep asking "And what does THAT give you?" until you reach a core value
4. Acknowledge the positive intention
5. Separate the intention from the behavior — the intention is valid, the behavior may need updating

### Category 2: PATTERNS FOR INCONGRUITY ("Parts" Conflicts)
*Use when someone is in internal conflict, self-sabotaging, or experiencing mixed feelings.*

**#11 Collapsing Anchors Pattern**
Purpose: Neutralize a negative state by collapsing it with a powerful positive state.
Steps:
1. Anchor a strong negative state on one knuckle/location
2. Break state
3. Anchor an even STRONGER positive state on a different location
4. Break state
5. Fire both anchors simultaneously
6. Hold until the integration completes (confusion, then calm)
7. Release the negative anchor first, then the positive
8. Test: try to access the old negative state — it should be neutralized or changed

**#12 Parts Negotiation Pattern**
Purpose: Resolve conflict between two internal "parts" that want different things.
Steps:
1. Identify Part A and Part B (the conflicting drives)
2. Ask each part: "What is your positive intention?"
3. Keep chunking up until both parts share a common higher intention
4. Ask each part if it would be willing to cooperate with the other to achieve the shared intention
5. Generate new behaviors that satisfy both parts
6. Future pace the integration

**#13 Six-Step Reframing Pattern**
Purpose: Change unwanted behavior while preserving its positive function.
Steps:
1. Identify the behavior to change
2. Establish communication with the part responsible ("Will the part that runs this behavior communicate with me?")
3. Separate behavior from intention — ask the part: "What is your positive intention?"
4. Access your creative part — generate 3+ new behaviors that fulfill the same intention
5. Ask the responsible part: "Will you accept these new choices?"
6. Ecology check — ask all parts if they object

**#14 Aligning Perceptual Positions Pattern**
Purpose: Gain wisdom by viewing a situation from multiple perspectives.
Steps:
1. First Position (Self): Fully associate into your own experience
2. Second Position (Other): Step into the other person's shoes completely
3. Third Position (Observer): View the interaction from a neutral distance
4. Fourth Position (System): See the larger system/context
5. Integrate insights from all positions
6. Choose your response from this enriched understanding

**#15 Agreement Frame Pattern**
Purpose: Move past disagreement by finding common ground.
Steps:
1. Instead of "but" use "and"
2. "I appreciate that... and..."
3. "I respect that... and..."
4. "I agree that... and..."
5. Find the shared value or outcome beneath the disagreement

**#16 Aligned Self Pattern**
Purpose: Align all neurological levels for congruent action.
Steps:
1. Environment: Where and when do you want this?
2. Behavior: What specifically will you do?
3. Capability: What skills/abilities support this?
4. Beliefs/Values: What do you believe about this? Why is it important?
5. Identity: Who are you as you do this?
6. Mission/Spirituality: What larger purpose does this serve?
7. Walk through each level, aligning them into one congruent direction

**#17 Resolving Internal Conflict Pattern**
Purpose: End the war between competing internal voices/drives.
Steps:
1. Identify the two conflicting parts
2. Give each a location in space (left hand/right hand, or spatial positions)
3. Have each part state its case fully
4. Find the positive intention of each
5. Identify the higher shared outcome
6. Negotiate: what would integration look like?
7. Bring the two together (hands together, or spatial merge)
8. Notice the new integrated feeling

**#18 Advanced Visual Squash Pattern**
Purpose: Integrate conflicting parts at a deep level using visual and kinesthetic processing.
Steps:
1. Place each part on a separate hand (visualize each)
2. Ask each part to look at the other and recognize what it needs from the other
3. Allow the hands to slowly come together at their own pace
4. As they merge, notice the new integrated image/feeling that emerges
5. Bring the integrated image into your body
6. Future pace

### Category 3: PATTERNS FOR IDENTITY AND "SELF"
*Use for self-concept issues, limiting beliefs about who you are, and building empowering self-images.*

**#19 Belief Change Pattern**
Purpose: Transform limiting beliefs into empowering ones.
Steps:
1. Identify the limiting belief
2. Identify the desired empowering belief
3. Find the submodality differences between a belief you doubt and one you're certain of
4. Map across: change the submodalities of the limiting belief to match "doubt"
5. Then change the submodalities of the empowering belief to match "certainty"
6. Test: how does the old belief feel now?

**#20 Dis-Identification Pattern**
Purpose: Separate your identity from a limiting label or role.
Steps:
1. Identify the limiting identification ("I AM [label]")
2. Recognize: you are not your behavior, emotions, or roles
3. Reframe: "I sometimes DO [behavior]" vs "I AM [label]"
4. Access a meta-position: observe the self that is observing
5. Choose: "I am the one who is aware of [behavior/emotion/role]"
6. Install the new self-concept

**#21 Re-Imprinting Pattern**
Purpose: Heal and update formative childhood experiences that shaped limiting beliefs.
Steps:
1. Identify the limiting belief and trace it back to its earliest memory (the "imprint")
2. Step out of the memory (dissociate) and observe it from a resource position
3. Identify what resources the younger you needed but didn't have
4. Fully access those resources NOW in your adult self
5. Step back into the memory WITH the resources and re-experience it
6. Notice how the belief changes when the younger you has what was needed
7. Bring the updated learning forward through your timeline to the present

**#22 Time-Line Pattern**
Purpose: Change your relationship with time to alter emotional responses to past/future events.
Steps:
1. Discover your time-line (how do you represent past, present, future spatially?)
2. Float above your time-line (meta-position)
3. Travel to the event you want to change
4. From above, add resources, change perspectives, or update meanings
5. Return to the present, noticing how the changes ripple forward
6. Future pace

**#23 Change Personal History Pattern**
Purpose: Update past memories with new resources so they no longer limit you.
Steps:
1. Identify the current limiting state/response
2. Anchor it, then trace it back through time to earlier instances
3. Find the earliest significant memory
4. Break state — access the resources you needed then
5. Anchor the resources strongly
6. Re-enter each past memory WITH the resources anchored
7. Notice how each memory updates and transforms
8. Return to the present and future pace

**#24 Swish Pattern**
Purpose: Redirect automatic negative responses toward a compelling positive self-image.
Steps:
1. Identify the trigger (what you see/hear just before the unwanted response)
2. Create a compelling image of who you want to become (the "desired self")
3. Make the trigger image big and bright
4. Place the desired self image small and dark in the corner
5. SWISH: simultaneously make the trigger shrink/darken while the desired self grows bright
6. Blank the screen
7. Repeat 5-7 times rapidly
8. Test: try to bring up the trigger — it should be difficult or automatically go to the desired self

**#25 Circle of Excellence Pattern**
Purpose: Create a portable resource state you can step into anytime.
Steps:
1. Imagine a circle on the floor in front of you (give it a color)
2. Recall a time of excellence — step into the circle at the peak of the state
3. Anchor the state to the circle
4. Step out, break state
5. Stack additional resource states into the same circle
6. Test: step into the circle — all resources should fire simultaneously
7. Future pace: imagine stepping into the circle in a challenging situation

**#26 Decision Destroyer Pattern**
Purpose: Undo a limiting decision that was made in the past.
Steps:
1. Identify the limiting decision
2. Float above your time-line
3. Go back to just before the decision was made
4. From this position, notice what you know NOW that you didn't know then
5. Make a new, more empowering decision from this wiser perspective
6. Let the new decision ripple forward through your time-line to the present
7. Notice what changes

**#27 Core Transformation Pattern**
Purpose: Transform limiting behaviors by accessing the deepest core state they're seeking.
Steps:
1. Identify an unwanted behavior or feeling
2. Ask the part responsible: "What do you want?"
3. When you get that, ask: "If you had that fully, what would that give you that's even more important?"
4. Keep asking until you reach a "core state" (usually: peace, oneness, being, love, OKness)
5. Invite the part to experience the core state FIRST, then notice how the original behavior transforms
6. Reverse the chain: bring the core state back through each level
7. Future pace with the core state present

**#28 The Meta-Transformation Pattern**
Purpose: Apply meta-state awareness to transform any experience.
Steps:
1. Identify the state you want to transform
2. Go meta: what do you think/feel ABOUT that state?
3. Apply a resource state TO the problem state (e.g., acceptance to anxiety)
4. Notice the transformation
5. Continue layering meta-states until the experience shifts completely

**#29 Making Peace with Your Parents Pattern**
Purpose: Resolve lingering resentment or pain from parental relationships.
Steps:
1. See your parent(s) as they were at the age when the painful events occurred
2. Consider: what was happening in THEIR life? What resources did THEY lack?
3. See them as children themselves — what happened to them?
4. Access compassion and understanding (not condoning)
5. From this understanding, choose what you want to keep and what to release
6. Create a new relationship frame going forward

**#30 Loving Yourself Pattern**
Purpose: Develop genuine self-acceptance and self-love.
Steps:
1. Think of someone you love unconditionally
2. Notice the submodalities of that love (how you represent it internally)
3. Now think of yourself
4. Apply those same submodalities to your self-image
5. Say to yourself: "I accept myself fully, including my imperfections"
6. Notice the shift in feeling
7. Practice daily until it becomes automatic

**#31 Self-Sufficiency Pattern**
Purpose: Build internal resources so you don't depend on external validation.
Steps:
1. Identify where you seek external approval
2. Ask: "What would I need to believe about myself to not need this?"
3. Access times when you felt completely self-sufficient
4. Amplify those states and anchor them
5. Future pace: imagine situations where you previously sought approval, now responding from self-sufficiency

**#32 Receiving Wisdom from Your Inner Sage Pattern**
Purpose: Access your own deep wisdom and intuition.
Steps:
1. Relax and go inside
2. Imagine meeting a wise version of yourself (older, wiser, having lived a full life)
3. Ask your inner sage the question you need answered
4. Listen with full attention — notice what comes (images, words, feelings)
5. Thank your inner sage
6. Bring the wisdom back to your present awareness
7. Decide how to act on it

### Category 4: PATTERNS FOR NEURO-LINGUISTIC STATES
*Use for emotional management, phobias, compulsions, and state control.*

**#33 Visual/Kinesthetic Dissociation Pattern (Phobia Cure)**
Purpose: Eliminate phobic responses by dissociating the visual memory from the kinesthetic response.
Steps:
1. Imagine sitting in a movie theater, watching yourself on the screen
2. Float up to the projection booth (double dissociation)
3. From the booth, watch the younger you on the screen go through the traumatic event — in black and white, fast forward
4. When the movie reaches a safe point AFTER the event, freeze frame
5. Now step INTO the screen at that safe end point
6. Run the movie BACKWARDS in full color, very fast (1-2 seconds)
7. Repeat 3-5 times
8. Test: try to access the phobic response — it should be significantly reduced or gone

**#34 Accessing and Managing Resourceful States Pattern**
Purpose: Develop the ability to choose your emotional state at will.
Steps:
1. Identify the resourceful state you want
2. Recall a time you experienced it fully
3. Step into the memory (associated, first person)
4. Amplify the submodalities (brighter, louder, more intense)
5. Anchor at peak
6. Practice accessing the state in various contexts
7. Chain multiple resource states for complex situations

**#35 State of Consciousness Awareness Pattern**
Purpose: Develop meta-awareness of your own states.
Steps:
1. Right now, notice what state you're in
2. How do you know? What are the signals?
3. Practice state-checking throughout the day
4. When you notice an unresourceful state, choose to shift
5. Build the habit of state awareness as a background process

**#36 "As If" Frame Pattern**
Purpose: Access resources by acting "as if" you already have them.
Steps:
1. Identify what you want to be able to do
2. Ask: "If I COULD do this, how would I be standing/breathing/thinking?"
3. Step into the "as if" physiology fully
4. Notice what becomes available from this state
5. Act from this state — the resources often become real through the acting

**#37 Chaining States Pattern**
Purpose: Create a sequence of states that leads from a stuck state to a desired state.
Steps:
1. Identify current stuck state (A)
2. Identify desired state (D)
3. Identify 2-3 intermediate states (B, C) that bridge the gap
4. Anchor each state in sequence (different locations)
5. Fire A → immediately fire B → C → D
6. Repeat until the chain runs automatically
7. Test: fire A alone — does it automatically lead to D?

**#38 Submodality Overlapping Pattern**
Purpose: Transfer the qualities of one representational system to another for richer experience.
Steps:
1. Start with the strongest representational system (e.g., visual)
2. Gradually add elements from another system (e.g., add sounds to the picture)
3. Then add the third system (e.g., add feelings)
4. Build rich, multi-sensory representations
5. Use this to enhance positive states or to access blocked representational systems

**#39 Threshold Pattern (Compulsion Blowout)**
Purpose: Push a compulsion past its threshold until it collapses.
Steps:
1. Identify the compulsion
2. Access the submodalities that drive it (usually a compelling image)
3. Amplify those submodalities beyond their natural threshold (make it TOO big, TOO bright, TOO fast)
4. Keep pushing until the representation "breaks" or becomes ridiculous
5. The compulsion should lose its power
6. Install a new, healthier response in its place

**#40 Transforming "Mistakes" into "Learnings"**
Purpose: Reframe failures as feedback and learning opportunities.
Steps:
1. Identify a "mistake" that still bothers you
2. Dissociate from it (watch it like a movie)
3. Ask: "What can I learn from this?"
4. Ask: "What would I do differently next time?"
5. Recode the memory as a "learning" — change the internal label
6. Notice how your feelings about it shift
7. Future pace with the new learning integrated

**#41 The Godiva Chocolate Pattern (Becoming Intentionally Compelled)**
Purpose: Make a desired behavior as compelling as something you're already compelled to do.
Steps:
1. Identify something you're already compelled to do (e.g., eating chocolate)
2. Notice the submodalities that make it compelling
3. Identify the behavior you WANT to be compelled to do
4. Map the compelling submodalities onto the desired behavior
5. Test: does the desired behavior now feel compelling?
6. Future pace

**#42 Decision-Making Pattern**
Purpose: Make clear decisions by accessing your values and criteria hierarchy.
Steps:
1. Identify the decision to be made
2. List all options
3. For each option, check against your top values/criteria
4. Notice which option aligns with the most important values
5. Check ecology
6. Commit and future pace

**#43 Pleasure Pattern**
Purpose: Amplify positive experiences and install them as accessible states.
Steps:
1. Recall a time of intense pleasure
2. Step fully into the memory
3. Amplify all submodalities (brighter, louder, more intense feelings)
4. Anchor at peak
5. Stack multiple pleasure experiences
6. Use the anchor to access pleasure states when needed

**#44 Reducing Enjoyment Pattern (Anti-Addiction Pattern)**
Purpose: Reduce the appeal of an addictive or unwanted pleasure.
Steps:
1. Identify the unwanted pleasure/addiction
2. Access the submodalities that make it appealing
3. Systematically reduce those submodalities (dimmer, quieter, further away)
4. Add unpleasant submodalities (bad smell, ugly color, grating sound)
5. Simultaneously amplify a healthier alternative
6. Test: does the old pleasure still appeal? It should be significantly reduced

**#45 Breaking Up Limiting Synesthesias Pattern**
Purpose: Disconnect automatic linkages between stimuli and responses that don't serve you.
Steps:
1. Identify the synesthesia (e.g., "seeing my boss → feeling small")
2. Notice the automatic linkage between the representational systems
3. Break the link by inserting a gap (interrupt the sequence)
4. Install a new, more useful connection
5. Test and future pace

**#46 Filing Away Memories as Part of One's Learning History**
Purpose: Take old memories that intrude on the present and file them appropriately in the past.
Steps:
1. Identify the intrusive memory
2. Extract the learning from it
3. Imagine your time-line
4. Place the memory appropriately in the past (with its learning attached)
5. Notice it receding to its proper place
6. Return to the present, lighter

### Category 5: PATTERNS FOR LANGUAGING AND RE-LANGUAGING
*Use for precision in communication, challenging limiting language, and empowering speech.*

**#47 Meta-Model Pattern**
Purpose: Challenge deletions, distortions, and generalizations in language to recover deeper structure.
Steps:
1. Listen for Meta-Model violations:
   - Deletions: "They don't understand" → "Who specifically? Understand what?"
   - Distortions: "He makes me angry" → "How specifically does he make you angry?"
   - Generalizations: "I always fail" → "Always? Every single time?"
2. Ask precision questions to recover the deleted/distorted information
3. Help the person create a more accurate map of reality

**#48 Meta-Model III Pattern**
Purpose: Advanced meta-modeling that addresses higher-level abstractions and presuppositions.
Steps:
1. Listen for complex equivalences ("X means Y")
2. Challenge cause-effect claims ("X causes Y" → "How specifically?")
3. Identify and challenge presuppositions
4. Explore mind-reads ("I know what they think" → "How do you know?")
5. Challenge lost performatives ("It's wrong to..." → "According to whom?")

**#49 Denominalizing Pattern**
Purpose: Turn frozen nouns (nominalizations) back into active processes.
Steps:
1. Identify nominalizations (abstract nouns that are actually processes): "depression," "failure," "relationship"
2. Turn them back into verbs: "How are you depressing yourself?" "How are you failing?" "How are you relating?"
3. This restores agency and choice to the person
4. Now they can change the PROCESS instead of being stuck with a THING

**#50 Problem Defining/Formulating Pattern**
Purpose: Redefine problems in ways that make them solvable.
Steps:
1. State the problem as currently defined
2. Challenge the frame: "Is this really the problem, or a symptom?"
3. Redefine in terms of process (what's happening) rather than identity (what you are)
4. Specify the desired state
5. Identify the gap between current and desired
6. Define the problem as the gap — now it's actionable

### Category 6: PATTERNS FOR THINKING PATTERNS (Meta-Programs & Cognitive Distortions)
*Use for understanding and shifting habitual thinking styles.*

**#51 Identifying and Pacing a Person's Meta-Programs**
Purpose: Recognize someone's habitual thinking patterns and communicate in their style.
Steps:
1. Listen for meta-program indicators in their language
2. Key meta-programs to identify:
   - Toward/Away From (motivated by goals vs. avoiding problems)
   - Internal/External reference (self-directed vs. other-directed)
   - Options/Procedures (choices vs. step-by-step)
   - Big Picture/Detail (global vs. specific)
   - Matcher/Mismatcher (sameness vs. difference)
3. Pace their meta-programs in your communication
4. Then lead toward more useful patterns when appropriate

**#52 Recognizing and Challenging Limiting Meta-Programs**
Purpose: Help someone see how their habitual thinking patterns limit them.
Steps:
1. Identify the meta-program that's causing problems in context
2. Demonstrate how it limits them in this specific situation
3. Show them the opposite meta-program and its advantages
4. Help them develop flexibility to use either as appropriate
5. Practice the new pattern in safe contexts

**#53 Meta-Programs Change Pattern**
Purpose: Shift a habitual meta-program to a more useful one.
Steps:
1. Identify the current limiting meta-program
2. Identify the desired meta-program
3. Find a context where you already USE the desired meta-program
4. Access that state fully
5. Anchor it
6. Fire the anchor in the context where you want the new meta-program
7. Practice until it becomes the new default in that context

**#54 Identifying and Disputing Cognitive Distortions**
Purpose: Recognize and challenge irrational thinking patterns.
Steps:
1. Identify the cognitive distortion:
   - All-or-nothing thinking
   - Overgeneralization
   - Mental filter (focusing on negatives)
   - Disqualifying the positive
   - Mind reading
   - Fortune telling
   - Catastrophizing
   - Emotional reasoning
   - Should statements
   - Labeling
   - Personalization
2. Challenge with evidence: "What's the evidence for and against this thought?"
3. Generate a more balanced, accurate thought
4. Notice the emotional shift

### Category 7: PATTERNS FOR MEANINGS AND SEMANTICS
*Use for reframing, value work, and changing the meaning of experiences.*

**#55 Content Reframing Pattern**
Purpose: Change the meaning of an experience by changing what you focus on.
Steps:
1. Identify the experience and its current meaning
2. Ask: "What else could this mean?"
3. Generate 3+ alternative meanings
4. Choose the most empowering meaning
5. Install it by rehearsing the new frame

**#56 Context Reframing Pattern**
Purpose: Find a context where the "problem" behavior is actually useful.
Steps:
1. Identify the behavior labeled as problematic
2. Ask: "In what context would this behavior be a valuable resource?"
3. Find at least 3 contexts where it's useful
4. Reframe: "So you have this ability — you just need to use it in the right context"
5. This preserves self-esteem while directing change

**#57 Submodalities Reframing Pattern**
Purpose: Change the meaning of an experience by changing its internal representation.
Steps:
1. Identify the limiting experience
2. Notice its submodalities (size, brightness, distance, etc.)
3. Experiment: change one submodality at a time and notice the effect on meaning
4. Find the "driver" submodality that most changes the meaning
5. Change it to create a more empowering meaning
6. Lock in the new representation

**#58 Six-Step Reframing as a Meta-States Pattern**
Purpose: Advanced version of six-step reframing using meta-state awareness.
Steps:
1. Identify the behavior
2. Go meta: what do you think/feel ABOUT this behavior?
3. Access the part's positive intention (meta-level)
4. From the meta-position, generate new choices
5. Get agreement from the meta-level
6. Ecology check from an even higher meta-level

**#59 Pulling Apart Belief Synesthesia Pattern**
Purpose: Disconnect beliefs that have been fused together inappropriately.
Steps:
1. Identify the fused belief (e.g., "money = evil" or "success = loneliness")
2. Recognize these are two separate concepts artificially linked
3. Find counter-examples that disprove the link
4. Separate them representationally (put them in different locations/submodalities)
5. Install new, more useful connections if desired

**#60 Establishing Your Value Hierarchy Pattern**
