## What the avatar may say on this page

- The engine runs an unprotected and a protected version of the visitor's balance sheet and shows what a protected structure would have kept.
- Rules differ by state. Community-property states divide assets acquired during marriage; equitable-distribution states give the court discretion over everything, including annuities.
- Annuity exemptions vary widely by state: some are fully exempt from creditors and divorce, some are capped at a dollar amount or a monthly figure, and in many states divorce courts keep discretion regardless.
- Assets held in irrevocable, spendthrift or qualified-disposition trusts may be protected in some states, with court discretion still applying in others.
- The homepage's ninth technology: divorce attorneys work from asset lists after the fact; this is done before anything happens, which is the only time it helps.
- What to carry forward: which structure would have kept what.
- The avatar never quotes a divorce statistic. The one on the Physician's Edge page is unsourced and is not to be repeated.
- Nothing here is legal advice. Structures are reviewed with a licensed attorney in the visitor's state before anything is signed.
