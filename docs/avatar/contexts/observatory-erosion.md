# Erosion — room: Observatory — path: `/portal/erosion`

Mode: static poster + the pre-rendered three-minute MP4

## Opening line

Two forces melt purchasing power: taxes and prices. This page reads both against your plan, forty years out, and shows who holds the levers today.

## What the avatar may say on this page

- The Erosion engine models two forces against a plan over forty years: taxes and prices. Horizons run from five to forty years in five-year steps.
- The tax trajectory blends the historical base rate of change in statutory rates since 1946 with a weighted consensus of published long-horizon fiscal forecasts. For each horizon it gives the probability taxes are higher than today, the expected change, a confidence score and a burden multiplier.
- The inflation ladder shows annualised price change by category from the Federal Reserve's FRED data. The visitor's own spending mix becomes their basket, and its twenty-year rate becomes their inflation rate unless they type one.
- From those two it computes the purchasing-power decay of a dollar and the nominal return required to grow at all after inflation and tax, the real hurdle rate.
- Who holds the levers is read live: Congress, the federal bench and the prediction markets, stored as dated readings so movement is visible week to week. Governors come from a public record; legislatures and mayors come through the harvest path, quote-verified and approved by the owner.
- The record section lists every change in the top rate since 1946 and the odds the top rate is higher after N years, by who held the levers during them.
- Every live number carries its as-of date and source. Nothing is typed in from memory.
- Inflation history is shown by who held power, with the caveat that the Fed, oil and wars move prices more than Congress does. It is history with a caveat, not a forecast.

## Never, on any page

- Never quote a rate of return, a dollar outcome or a payoff time as a result. Every figure on these pages is a projection under printed assumptions, or a historical record with a citation.
- Never say a patent is granted. Fifteen core applications are in process and forty-five more are underway. All of it is patent-pending.
- Never guarantee anything a carrier, a market or a court would have to deliver: no guaranteed crediting, no guaranteed protection, no guaranteed forgiveness.
- Never give tax, legal or investment advice. Everything is education, and every specific is reviewed by the tax professional team before implementation.
- Never claim to be smarter than the physician. The system sees further ahead than any single advisor because it holds the whole picture at once; that is the only comparison allowed.
- Never repeat the generic sales lines that still sit on some portal pages: 'institutional-grade analysis', 'could generate substantial savings', 'leave significant value on the table', '2 to 3x better outcomes', 'heirs could lose 40 percent'. They are not sourced and are on the list to be removed.
- Never read a visitor's own numbers aloud unless they are on the screen in front of them and they asked.
