### Detailed Findings

**AQAL Complementary Mode** — MOSTLY SOUND (minor issues)

- Research support: Vygotsky's Zone of Proximal Development supports pairing growth edges with strengths. Dryer & Horowitz (1997) and McPherson et al. (2001) suggest complementarity is effective but requires a strong baseline of similarity (homophily) to prevent friction, indicating the 65/35 split might under-weight compatibility.
- Issues: The 65/35 weight split may overemphasize differences, risking communication breakdowns; consider a more balanced 50/50 split or dynamic weighting. The 5 altitude lines provide a solid worldview baseline, but adding emotional or cognitive lines could better ensure foundational compatibility. Ensure the 'bottom third' logic does not pair users with unbridgeable developmental gaps.

**Resonance Mode** — MOSTLY SOUND (minor issues)

- Research support: Homophily research (McPherson, Smith-Lovin & Cook 2001) strongly supports similarity for peer connection and trust building. Focusing on shared strengths (peaks) aligns with positive psychology (Clifton & Harter, 2003), as shared high-competence areas foster mutual understanding and resonance.
- Issues: The 0.82 peak threshold is arbitrary and may exclude users if scores are normally distributed; a dynamic threshold (e.g., top 15% or +1 SD) is safer. Normalizing breadth to 5 assumes users have many peaks, which could artificially deflate scores for generalists. Consider adjusting the breadth cap based on empirical user data and softening the 0.82 cutoff.

**Thresholds** — MOSTLY SOUND (minor issues)

- Research support: Vygotsky's Zone of Proximal Development supports requiring absolute mastery (0.82) for a partner to scaffold growth. Tertile splits (1/3) are standard in ipsative psychometric scoring to identify relative strengths/weaknesses. However, homophily research (McPherson et al., 2001) warns that overly restrictive similarity thresholds can isolate users.
- Issues: A fixed absolute threshold of 0.82 may be too restrictive if scores are normally distributed, potentially resulting in zero matches for average users. Recommend making `strengthAbs` dynamic based on population percentiles (e.g., 80th percentile) rather than a hardcoded value. Additionally, empirically test if 5 shared peaks is too rare to achieve in practice, adjusting `breadthNorm` based on actual user data distributions.

**Generational Adjustment** — MOSTLY SOUND (minor issues)

- Research support: Homophily research (McPherson, Smith-Lovin & Cook, 2001) strongly supports age similarity for peer connection (resonance). Mentoring literature (Kram, 1985; Murphy, 2012) supports cross-generational relationships for complementary growth, though extreme gaps can impede mutual understanding.
- Issues: An 8-point weight is a reasonable secondary factor, but maximizing the complementary bonus at 3+ generations apart risks severe communication barriers. Recommend peaking the complementary bonus at a 1-2 generation gap (typical mentor-mentee dynamic) rather than 3+. Ensure this adjustment acts as a tie-breaker and does not override core cognitive alignment.

**AQAL Altitude Lines** — QUESTIONABLE (The selection of only 5 lines appears arbitrary and excludes key developmental drivers like Meta-Cognitive and Volitional lines.)

- Research support: Research in adult development (e.g., Kegan's constructive-developmental theory) and self-regulated learning emphasizes meta-cognition and volition as critical for developmental growth. While intrapersonal and reflective lines are supported by Vygotsky's zone of proximal development for growth partnerships, excluding meta-cognitive limits the assessment of a user's capacity for self-directed evolution.
- Issues: The exclusion of Meta-Cognitive and Volitional lines risks matching individuals who lack the self-awareness or drive to sustain a growth partnership. Recommend expanding the altitude lines to include Meta-Cognitive and Volitional to better capture developmental capacity. Additionally, validate this selection empirically by tracking which lines most strongly predict successful peer connections over time.

**Honest Framing** — SOUND

- Research support: Finkel et al. (2012) on online dating algorithms confirms that no mathematical model has been scientifically validated to predict long-term relationship or partnership success, supporting the platform's transparent disclaimer.
- Issues: The transparent framing is ethically sound and protects the platform from overpromising results. The primary risk is that users may ignore the disclaimer if it is buried in fine print. It is recommended to prominently display this framing during user onboarding and when presenting match suggestions to effectively manage expectations.

**Bottleneck Roles** — MOSTLY SOUND (minor issues)

- Research support: Kremer's (1993) O-Ring theory supports multiplicative failure models. Woolley et al. (2010) on collective intelligence validates that social sensitivity (empathy) acts as a critical node, where deficits degrade overall team performance. Goldratt's (1984) Theory of Constraints and Liebig's Law are correctly adapted from their source literature.
- Issues: The conceptual mapping is strong, and classifying Interpersonal and Empathic lines as O-Ring aligns well with team dynamics research where social deficits degrade overall synergy. However, Liebig and Theory of Constraints may be mathematically redundant if both simply cap output based on the lowest score. Recommendation: Ensure the algorithm mathematically distinguishes between these mechanisms (e.g., multiplicative degradation vs. hard cap) and consider merging Liebig and TOC if their mathematical execution is identical.

**AQAL Axis Modes** — QUESTIONABLE (Claiming genuine independence from g for Humor/Adversarial contradicts psychometrics, and mixing 5 measurement modes creates normalization risks)

- Research support: Carroll's Three-Stratum Theory (1993) and Greengross & Miller (2011) show cognitive tasks like humor correlate with general intelligence (g), contradicting the independence claim. Beck & Cowan's Spiral Dynamics (1996) supports treating 'stance' as value systems, justifying their exclusion from rarity metrics.
- Issues: The claim that Humor and Adversarial lines are completely independent of g is psychometrically flawed and should be revised to reflect lower, but non-zero, g-loadings. Mixing five distinct measurement modes (percentiles, distances, stages) creates severe normalization risks when calculating aggregate match scores. Standardize all modes to a common mathematical scale before matching, and empirically validate the g-loadings of the excluded lines.

### Lines with Weak Support (Potential Issues)

**Philosophical intelligence line** — WEAK SUPPORT

- Distinctness: OVERLAPPING (with Logical and Existential lines)
- Measurement: Defining Issues Test (moral reasoning), Watson-Glaser Critical Thinking Appraisal, and Gardner's research on Existential Intelligence.
- Issue: Philosophical reasoning is typically viewed as an application of logical, verbal, and existential capacities rather than a standalone intelligence. Including it creates significant redundancy with the Logical and Existential lines. It represents a domain of knowledge and skill rather than a distinct cognitive faculty.

**Tactical intelligence line** — WEAK SUPPORT

- Distinctness: OVERLAPPING (with Strategic and Volitional lines)
- Measurement: Sternberg's Triarchic Theory (Practical Intelligence), Executive Function assessments (e.g., BRIEF), and Big Five Conscientiousness scales (NEO-PI-R)
- Issue: Tactical intelligence is not a recognized standalone cognitive capacity in mainstream psychometrics, but rather a contextual application of other skills. It is typically subsumed under practical intelligence, executive functioning, or the personality trait of conscientiousness. It heavily overlaps with both strategic planning and volitional execution.

**Architectural intelligence line** — WEAK SUPPORT

- Distinctness: OVERLAPPING (with Spatial and Systematic)
- Measurement: WAIS Block Design, Mental Rotation Test, and Bennett Mechanical Comprehension Test (which measure underlying spatial and mechanical traits, not architectural intelligence specifically)
- Issue: This represents a domain-specific skill or occupational application rather than a fundamental cognitive capacity. It heavily conflates spatial visualization with systems thinking, making it redundant as a standalone intelligence line.

**Parenting intelligence line** — WEAK SUPPORT

- Distinctness: OVERLAPPING (with Interpersonal and Emotional intelligence lines)
- Measurement: Baumrind's parenting styles research, Parenting Stress Index (PSI), Adult-Adolescent Parenting Inventory (AAPI), and developmental psychology research on attachment.
- Issue: Parenting is generally considered a role-specific skill domain or behavioral application rather than a fundamental cognitive capacity. It heavily relies on broader constructs like interpersonal intelligence, empathy, and emotional regulation, making its inclusion as a standalone intelligence problematic.

**Seduction intelligence line** — WEAK SUPPORT

- Distinctness: OVERLAPPING (with Social, Emotional, and Interpersonal intelligence lines)
- Measurement: Mating Intelligence Scale (Geher & Kaufman, 2007) and evolutionary psychology research programs on mating intelligence (Miller, 2000)
- Issue: This construct is typically viewed as a domain-specific application of social and emotional intelligence rather than a distinct cognitive capacity. Furthermore, the term 'seduction' carries controversial, manipulative connotations that are often associated with Dark Triad personality traits rather than a standard intelligence.

**Community-Founding intelligence line** — WEAK SUPPORT

- Distinctness: OVERLAPPING (with Interpersonal Intelligence, Social Intelligence, and Leadership)
- Measurement: Multifactor Leadership Questionnaire (MLQ), Social Capital assessments, and Organizational Behavior research on transformational leadership.
- Issue: This represents a specific behavioral application or skill domain rather than a fundamental cognitive capacity. It heavily overlaps with established constructs like interpersonal intelligence and social intelligence, confusing an outcome with an underlying psychological trait.

### Verdict on the 32-Line Model

The model contains:
- **12 well-established lines** with strong psychometric traditions (Logical, Spatial, Linguistic, Interpersonal, Empathic, Musical, Kinesthetic, Interoceptive, Resilient, Volitional, Meta-Cognitive, Intrapersonal)

The 6 weakly supported lines are notably the same ones classified as "stance" lines in the axis modes system — suggesting the platform already acknowledges their different status by measuring them differently (developmental stage rather than percentile). This is an appropriate design decision.

## Overall Platform Assessment

### Strengths

1. **Honest framing throughout.** The matching algorithm explicitly states no validated model exists. The axis modes system distinguishes between what can be percentiled and what cannot. The research library labels debunked claims. This intellectual honesty is the platform's moat.

2. **Two-layer prescription design.** The therapy mapping table handles the 21 clinical lines; the wider 6,246-cluster library handles the 11 gap lines. This is architecturally sound.

3. **Stage framework is empirically grounded.** All 9 stages come from validated instruments (SCTi-MAP) with published population data. Two data entry errors exist but the framework itself is solid.

4. **Matching algorithm is well-designed.** Both modes (complementary and resonance) have clear mathematical logic with reasonable weights. The generational adjustment is a thoughtful addition.

### Issues Requiring Attention

1. **Behavioral Activation rated "Emerging"** — This is a significant error affecting prescription quality. Should be Strong.
2. **Stage 3/4 population estimate wrong** — Shows 30% but should be 38%. Data entry error.
3. **19 of 29 keystone practices use non-AQAL line categories** — Needs systematic remapping.
4. **Altitude lines selection appears arbitrary** — Only 5 lines used for developmental compatibility; rationale unclear.
5. **6 intelligence lines have weak empirical support** — But already handled appropriately via stance classification.
6. **Mechanism texts scrambled** — Fixed in this audit; needs to be applied to production code.

### Bottom Line

The platform is architecturally sound, intellectually honest, and largely well-calibrated. The issues found are data entry errors and classification inconsistencies, not fundamental design flaws. The adversarial audit loop (Manus challenges, Claude audits, survivors get upgraded) is genuinely the best quality-assurance process available for a system of this complexity.
