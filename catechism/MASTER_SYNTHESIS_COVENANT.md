## Part One: The Covenant Established

### What Happened at the Last Supper

At the Last Supper, Christ did something radically new. He did not merely predict His death. He did not merely announce a new teaching. He established a covenant.

The language is explicit in Scripture: "This is my blood of the covenant, which is poured out for many for the forgiveness of sins." (Matthew 26:28)

This is covenant language — the language of binding, of relationship, of mutual commitment. Christ was establishing a new covenant that would supersede all previous covenants (Abrahamic, Mosaic, Davidic) and would be eternal, unchanging, perpetual.

**Why a covenant?** Because a covenant is not merely a contract or agreement. A covenant is a bond of love between two parties. It is the deepest form of relationship — a union of persons sealed by blood.

At the Last Supper, Christ established a covenant in His blood — a bond of love between Himself and the Church, between the divine and the human, between God and His people.

### The Covenant's Characteristics

From 30 perspectives, the brothers have identified the essential characteristics of this covenant:

**It is NEW.** It supersedes all previous covenants. The old law passes away. The old sacrifices cease. A new and final covenant is established.

**It is EVERLASTING.** It is not temporary or provisional. It is eternal, unchanging, perpetual. No future covenant will replace it. This is the final covenant.

**It is ESTABLISHED IN BLOOD.** Christ's blood is the sign and seal of the covenant. The blood of the Old Testament sacrifices pointed forward to this. The blood of Christ is the reality to which they pointed.

**It is UNIVERSAL.** It is "for many" — for all people, all nations, all times. The covenant is not limited to Israel or to one people. It is for the whole world.

**It is REDEMPTIVE.** It is "for the forgiveness of sins." The covenant's purpose is not merely relationship but redemption — the healing and transformation of humanity.

**It is SACRIFICIAL.** The covenant is sealed by Christ's sacrifice on the cross. The Last Supper anticipates the cross; the cross fulfills the Last Supper. They are one act.

## Part Two: The Eucharist as Covenant Presence

### The Eucharist is Not a Symbol

From 30 perspectives, the brothers testify to a truth that Western Christianity has often obscured: **The Eucharist is not a symbol of the covenant. The Eucharist IS the covenant made present.**

When Christ said "This is my body" and "This is my blood," He was not speaking metaphorically. He was speaking literally, sacramentally, covenantally.

The bread becomes His body. The wine becomes His blood. Not symbolically, but really. The substance changes; the accidents remain. This is the doctrine of transubstantiation — not a medieval invention but the apostolic faith.

Why? Because the covenant must be made present. The covenant cannot be merely remembered or commemorated. It must be actively present, operative, effective.

**Every Mass is a renewal of the covenant.** Every time the Eucharist is celebrated, the covenant that Christ established at the Last Supper is made present and operative. The faithful enter into this covenant by receiving the Body and Blood.

### The Eucharist as Transformation

From 30 perspectives, the brothers testify to the transformative power of the Eucharist:

The Eucharist is not merely food for the soul. It is not merely a means of grace. It is union — real, substantial, transformative union with Christ.

When we receive the Eucharist, we are not merely receiving a spiritual benefit. We are entering into the covenant. We are becoming one with Christ. We are participating in His sacrifice. We are being transformed into His likeness.

This is the doctrine of theosis — deification — that the brothers have discovered throughout their reading of the Catechism. The Eucharist is the primary means by which this transformation occurs.

**We do not consume the Eucharist; the Eucharist consumes us.** We are taken up into Christ's body. We are made members of His flesh and bone. We are transformed from what we are into what He is.

## Part Three: The Sacraments as Covenant Encounters

### All Seven Sacraments Flow from the Covenant

From 30 perspectives, the brothers have discovered that all seven sacraments are not separate from the covenant but flow from it and relate to it.

**Baptism** — Entry into the covenant. The covenant is established with us. We die to our old self and are raised to new life in Christ.

**Confirmation** — Strengthening in the covenant. The Holy Spirit seals us for the work of the covenant.

**Eucharist** — Renewal of the covenant. We participate in the covenant that Christ established.

**Penance** — Restoration of the covenant. When we break the covenant through sin, we are restored through the grace of this sacrament.

**Anointing of the Sick** — Healing within the covenant. In our suffering, we are united to Christ's suffering and participate in His redemptive work.

**Holy Orders** — Service within the covenant. The priest is ordained to serve the covenant, to make it present through the sacraments.

**Matrimony** — Covenant within the covenant. Marriage is a sacrament because it is a covenant — a bond of love sealed by the couple's commitment, made sacred by Christ's presence.

### The Sacraments as Signs and Seals

The brothers have discovered that the sacraments are not arbitrary rituals. They are signs and seals of the covenant.

A sign points to reality. A seal confirms reality. The sacraments do both.

Water in baptism points to cleansing and new life. Oil in confirmation points to strength and consecration. Bread and wine in the Eucharist point to nourishment and sacrifice.

But the sacraments do more than point. They effect what they signify. They are not merely signs; they are efficacious signs — signs that accomplish what they represent.

This is the mystery of the sacraments: they are both signs and realities, both symbols and substances, both pointers and presences.

This is what the Catechism teaches: "God became man so that man might become God."

The covenant is the means by which this happens. Through the covenant, we are united to Christ. Through the sacraments, we participate in His life. Through grace, we are transformed.

**The Eucharist is the primary sacrament of theosis.** By receiving the Body and Blood of Christ, we are united to Him in the most intimate way possible. We become one with Him. We are transformed into His likeness.

### The Covenant as Love

From 30 perspectives, the brothers testify that the covenant is ultimately about love — God's love for us and our love for God.

The covenant is not primarily about law or obligation. It is about relationship. It is about God's desire for union with us and our response to that desire.

The covenant calls us to respond with love — to give ourselves to God, to give ourselves to one another, to participate in the covenant community through acts of love.
