The load-bearing four. If you only fix a handful before traffic arrives, make them these: the database opens a single connection with no pool (#23); 39 tables carry only 6 indexes and none of them on a lookup column (#1); the rate limiter lives in one process's memory and wipes itself clean at 50,000 keys (#31, #32); and the Stripe webhook verifies signatures but never deduplicates, so one retried delivery charges or grants twice (#74). Everything else is degradation. These four are outages and money.

Database and data layer 01–20

The largest cluster, and where thousands of users will hurt first. Verified against drizzle/schema.ts and server/db.ts.

Connection handling and caching 21–32

Nothing shared sits between the app and its data. Every instance is on its own.

  1. 21No cache layer of any kindNo Redis, memcached, or in-process LRU anywhere in server/.Critical
  2. 22Scoring recomputed on every requestNo memoization on deterministic scoring or profile derivation.High
  3. 23No database connection poolserver/db.ts calls drizzle(process.env.DATABASE_URL) with no pool, max, or timeout config. Concurrent requests serialize.Critical
  4. 24No pool sizing tied to instance countOnce pooling exists, max × instances must stay under the database's connection ceiling.High
  5. 25No Cache-Control on API responsesOnly two files reference cache headers: productRoutes.ts and _core/storageProxy.ts.High
  6. 26No ETag or conditional requestsEvery fetch transfers a full body even when unchanged.Medium
  7. 27Research corpus re-fetched per visitor6,246 clusters through /manus-storage on every Research Library visit; storageProxy sets no long-lived immutable cache.High
  8. 28No CDN in front of managed storageCorpus, founder photo and uploads all served through the app process.High
  9. 29Storage proxy is a throughput bottleneckserver/_core/storageProxy.ts streams every asset through Node rather than redirecting to signed URLs.High
  10. 30No stale-while-revalidate on static routesMarketing and archetype pages are cacheable but uncached.Medium
  11. 31Rate limiter is per-instance memoryserver/rateLimit.ts holds a module-level Map. Two instances mean double the effective limit; it resets on every deploy.Critical
  12. 32Rate limiter flushes every limit at 50,000 keysrateLimit.ts: if (windows.size > 50_000) windows.clear(). An attacker who inflates the map clears everyone's counter, including their own.Critical

Concurrency and horizontal scale 33–45

Several modules assume exactly one process. That assumption breaks the moment you add a second.

  1. 33Nine scheduled jobs have no distributed lockserver/scheduledJobs.ts registers 9 jobs; on multiple instances each fires per instance — duplicate emails, duplicate deletions.Critical
  2. 34Audio retention job can delete concurrentlyscheduledAudioRetention runs bounded deletes with no lock; two runs race.High
  3. 35No graceful shutdownNo SIGTERM handler draining in-flight requests; a deploy cuts live assessments mid-write.High
  4. 36Readiness and liveness are the same endpointproductRoutes.ts:67 — one /health returning ok:true. It does not check the database, so a DB-down instance still receives traffic.Critical
  5. 37No concurrency cap on LLM callsUnbounded parallel invokeLLM under load hits provider limits and stalls the pool.High
  6. 38No circuit breaker on providersA slow LLM or STT provider holds every request thread until timeout.High
  7. 39No timeout on outbound fetchesTranscription downloads audio with plain fetch and no AbortSignal.High
  8. 40No idempotency on assessment submissionA double-tap or retry creates two assessments.High
  9. 41Founding-spot allocation can oversellrateLimit.ts describes a 10,000-spot pool; without a transactional counter or unique constraint two requests can claim the last spot.Critical
  10. 42No background job queueNo BullMQ or equivalent — scoring, transcription and video analysis run inside the request.High
  11. 43Long tasks block the request cycleVideo and voice analysis inline means a slow provider ties up a connection for its duration.High
  12. 44No backpressure or load sheddingNothing rejects early when the process is saturated; requests queue until timeout.Medium
  13. 45No documented autoscaling policyNo thresholds, min/max instances, or scaling signal defined.Medium
